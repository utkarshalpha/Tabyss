# Users, Jobs to Be Done, and Benefits

## Primary audience

People who do meaningful work or study in Chrome but lose attention, context, or wellbeing inside the same browser.

## Primary user segments

### 1. Knowledge worker

- Context: Developer, analyst, designer, marketer, founder, researcher, writer.
- Pain: Productive and distracting sites exist in the same window; switching destroys momentum.
- Value: Plan-linked focus, relevant tabs, interruption recovery, and explainable work patterns.

### 2. Student and exam learner

- Pain: Research, video lessons, chat, entertainment, and short-form media blur together.
- Value: Study profiles, course-aware categorization, Pomodoro/flow cycles, exam plans, and friend focus rooms.

### 3. Freelancer or creator

- Pain: Needs flexible work boundaries, project attribution, and protection from overwork.
- Value: Project-linked sessions, client-safe local history, shutdown routines, and exportable summaries.

### 4. Executive-function support seeker

- Pain: Automatic opens, task initiation difficulty, time blindness, tab overload, and harsh blockers that are abandoned.
- Value: Low-friction start, visible time, gentle escalation, body-doubling presence, recovery instead of punishment, and reduced setup burden.

### 5. Privacy-conscious productivity user

- Pain: Wants insight but will not upload detailed browsing history.
- Value: Local-first analytics, clear permissions, selective connections, encrypted export, and verifiable data control.

## Secondary users

- Remote workers protecting work/life boundaries.
- Households coordinating device-free routines without monitoring domains.
- Small accountability groups.
- Users who mainly want eye, water, movement, and digital-sunset routines.

## Explicitly excluded target behavior

- Employer surveillance.
- Covert parental monitoring.
- Public productivity ranking based on raw browser hours.
- Users expecting OS-wide or mobile-app control from the Chrome extension alone.

## Core jobs to be done

| Situation | Job | Desired outcome | V2 response |
|---|---|---|---|
| I begin a browser task | Help me state what I intend to finish | Clear task and time boundary | Quick Plan and prepared workspace |
| I need uninterrupted work | Protect the relevant browser context | Fewer avoidable switches | Focus session, allowlist, parked tabs |
| I open a distracting site automatically | Create a moment to choose | Conscious continuation or exit | Mindful pause and intention prompt |
| A short visit becomes long | Help me notice and recover | Return without shame | Re-intervention and one-click recovery |
| My tabs become unmanageable | Preserve useful context without clutter | Clean browser and recoverable work | Spaces, snooze, grouping, checkpoint |
| My categories are wrong | Let me correct the model | Fair scoring and insights | In-context correction and rule scope |
| I feel productive but exhausted | Protect breaks and shutdown | Sustainable work | Recovery plans and overwork guardrails |
| I review my week | Explain what changed and why | One useful next adjustment | Weekly review and experiment |
| I use multiple devices or tools | Keep chosen configuration consistent | Continuity without data surprise | Optional scoped connection |

## Functional benefits

- Faster task start.
- Fewer unplanned site opens.
- Fewer context switches.
- Easier recovery from drift.
- Cleaner tab state.
- Better awareness of time and attention.
- Better alignment between activity and personal intent.
- More consistent breaks and shutdown.
- Private, correctable evidence of improvement.

## Emotional benefits

- Control without punishment.
- Clarity instead of guilt.
- Trust that private activity is not being monetized.
- Relief that unfinished browser context can be safely restored.
- Motivation from personality, progress, and personal bests.
- Confidence that rest is allowed.

## Social benefits

- Accountability without exposing browsing history.
- Ability to focus beside a friend.
- Shared goals expressed as completion or consistency, not surveillance.
- Celebrations that do not reveal sites or exact schedules.

## Anti-benefits to avoid

The product must not create:

- More configuration than value.
- A second task manager users must maintain.
- Notification fatigue.
- Anxiety from constant scoring.
- False precision from sparse data.
- Pressure to maximize working hours.
- Social shame or coercion.
- Permission fear from features the user did not enable.

## Activation moment

The recommended activation moment is not installation or the first dashboard view. A user activates after:

1. Tabyss establishes enough baseline to provide a credible observation.
2. The user chooses one desired outcome.
3. Tabyss supports one successful intentional session or recovery.
4. The product explains that outcome clearly.

## Magic moments

1. Trust: the permission/data receipt is simpler and narrower than expected.
2. Recognition: the first insight accurately describes a familiar pattern.
3. Action: starting focus also prepares the needed tabs.
4. Recovery: the user exits an unplanned session after a timely nudge.
5. Proof: the weekly review shows a personally meaningful improvement.
6. Delight: the persona or Wrapped reflects the week without exposing private details.
